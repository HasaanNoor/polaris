# Methodology Charter

## Purpose

This charter defines enforceable research principles for Polaris. It applies to agents, analytical services, artifacts, reports, and future LLM integrations.

## Core Rules

- Evidence before explanation.
- Correlation is not causation.
- AI assists research but does not replace statistical inference.
- Every quantitative claim must be traceable to data and deterministic analysis.
- Descriptive, correlational, predictive, quasi-experimental, experimental, and synthesized evidence must be distinguished.
- Uncertainty, effect sizes, assumptions, limitations, provenance, and practical significance must be reported.
- Missing data must never be silently imputed.
- Imputed and transformed observations must remain identifiable and traceable.
- Statistical significance alone is not sufficient evidence.
- Lack of statistical significance does not establish absence of an effect.
- Causal language requires an explicit and defensible identification strategy.
- Reproducibility is a first-class system requirement.
- Conflicting evidence must be represented rather than suppressed.
- Neutrality means faithfully representing evidence and uncertainty, not treating unsupported claims as equally credible.

## Distinct Assessment Dimensions

Data quality: source credibility, coverage, definitions, missingness, revisions, measurement, comparability, and provenance.

Statistical confidence: estimation uncertainty, sample size, model assumptions, diagnostics, multiple comparisons, and sensitivity.

Evidence quality: study design, source transparency, measurement validity, risk of bias, and consistency across evidence.

Causal identification strength: explicit design, assumptions, threats to validity, and whether alternative explanations are addressed.

External validity: whether findings generalize across populations, places, time periods, institutions, and measurement systems.

Practical significance: whether an estimated relationship is substantively meaningful, not merely statistically detectable.

## Enforcement

Agents and reports must fail validation when they omit required uncertainty, provenance, limitations, or causal-identification assessment for claims that require them.
